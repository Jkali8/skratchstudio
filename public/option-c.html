Fig. 01The chair, mid-cut. Every fade is built by eye, never guessed.

"We don't rush the clippers, and we don't do template cuts. Every head gets read before a single line is set."

Skratch Studio sits on a quiet street in Vilnius, behind a black door and a chrome mark. Inside, the room is deliberately calm — no noise, no upsell, just the chair and the work.

The method is simple to describe and hard to do: read the head, agree the shape, then build it slowly. A skin fade is a gradient drawn by hand; a beard sculpt is geometry; a hot-towel shave is a ritual that refuses to be hurried.

What you leave with should still look right on day ten — that's the only test that matters. Anything that survives a week of weather, sleep and bad mirrors is a cut worth paying for.

This first issue is the studio on paper: the menu, the method, and the people who keep the clippers sharp.

Sharp fades, sculpted beards, and a chair that's yours for the hour.
